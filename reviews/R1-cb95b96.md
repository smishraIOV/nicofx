# Critical review — FX Net / NicoFX

Reviewed (by Opus) at commit `cb95b96`, focus on **business logic** (settlement semantics, economics, incentives,
operational safety) rather than Solidity style. Every claim marked *(verified)* was reproduced against
this tree with a throwaway Foundry test; the baseline suite (36 tests) passes unmodified.

Where a finding is already tracked in [`ideation.md`](../ideation.md) I say so — the point of this document
is the delta, plus a re-ranking of what is already known.

---

## 1. Summary

The protocol is well-scoped and unusually well-documented for a prototype. The reframe from
"non-USD stablecoin" to "permissioned enterprise FX netting" is the right call, the per-bucket
collateral isolation is clean, and `eliminated` is the correct thing to measure.

The gap is that **the netting engine implements the happy path of a settlement system and none of the
failure path**. A real netting facility is mostly defined by what happens when a participant does not
deliver: unwind, retry, partial settlement, close-out. `NettingSession` has no such procedure, and the
result is not a stuck session but a permanently bricked contract holding other people's money
(finding **C1**). That is the single most important thing to fix, ahead of everything in ideation.md's
Phase B.

Second, the mint/redeem pair prices at a single oracle mid with **no spread and no mint fee**, which
hands allowlisted users a free option on the operator's buffer worth ~4.7% per 5% FX move
(finding **C2**). ideation.md tracks "oracle stale/deviation guards" as a data-quality item; it is
actually the protocol's largest direct capital leak, and deviation guards alone do not close it.

Third, the headline value metric (`eliminated`) is self-reportable by any single allowlisted party
(finding **H1**). That matters now for credibility and matters a lot if Phase D item 10 ever bills on it.

Severity ranking used below: **C**ritical (loses funds or bricks the system), **H**igh
(economically exploitable or breaks a stated guarantee), **M**edium, **L**ow / hygiene.

Two things worth stating plainly rather than burying:

- **C2 is not fully closed, and cannot be by pricing alone.** One rate per window removes the
  *risk-free* same-window round trip, and two-way fees mean a round trip costs
  `mintFeeBps + redeemFeeBps`. But a holder who keeps inventory across a rate change still profits
  from a favourable move at the buffer's expense: measured at ~4.47% on a 5% move, against ~4.74%
  before. That residual is inherent to redeeming at an oracle rate and belongs to
  `../devaluation-risk.md`; the real controls are per-bucket caps and a coverage breaker (ideation.md
  Phase B), which are still open.
- **H1 and H2 are bounded by what a chain can verify.** Consent and references stop one-sided and
  duplicate inflation, but two colluding allowlisted addresses can still generate `eliminated`
  volume, and on-chain acknowledgement is not the same thing as an enforceable master netting
  agreement. Both are now honestly labelled rather than implied to be trustless.

Test suite: **98 tests, all passing** (was 36). Verified end-to-end against a live Anvil chain:
the readme triangle settles with creditors paid 60.0000 and 30.0000 CLP at face value, the debtor
funding 90.45, and the contract retaining exactly the 0.45 fee; the failure path recovers a
depositor in full and the next session opens normally.

**Deployment note:** the `NettingSession` storage model changed to session-keyed mappings. These are
UUPS proxies, but an in-place upgrade over an older deployment would corrupt state — deploy fresh.

## 2. Critical

### R1-C1. A locked session with one non-delivering debtor permanently traps escrow and bricks the contract

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:136-144`, `:191-207`, `:209-234`

Once `lockSession` runs, the only exits are "everybody funds" or "nobody has funded yet":

- `claim` requires `currencyFullyFunded(currency)` (`:213`), so one short debtor blocks **every** creditor
  in that currency.
- `cancelSession` reverts with `EscrowNotEmpty` if *any* currency has received *any* funding (`:138-141`).
- `openSession` requires phase ∈ {Idle, Settled, Cancelled} (`:112`), so a stuck `Locked` session blocks
  all future sessions too.
- There is no `refund`, `unwind`, `withdrawFunding`, or timeout anywhere in the contract.

Net effect: the tokens of the debtors who *did* deliver are unrecoverable, and the netting service is
dead for all participants. The only escape is a UUPS upgrade by the session admin — i.e. the recovery
procedure for a routine settlement failure is an emergency contract upgrade.

Reproduction (Alice owes 100 and delivers, Bob owes 50 and does not):

```solidity
s.postObligation(alice, carol, Currency.CLP, 100e18);
s.postObligation(bob,   carol, Currency.CLP,  50e18);  // bob holds no inventory
s.lockSession();
vm.prank(alice); s.fund(Currency.CLP);                 // 100 CLP now in escrow

vm.prank(carol); s.claim(Currency.CLP);   // reverts CurrencyNotFullyFunded
vm.prank(admin); s.cancelSession();       // reverts EscrowNotEmpty
vm.prank(admin); s.openSession();         // reverts InvalidPhase
// alice balance: 0. escrow: 100e18. no code path returns it.
```

This is also a **griefing vector, not just an accident**. `postObligation` lets any allowlisted party
name *itself* debtor against any allowlisted creditor (`:177`). So one participant can post a large
obligation just before lock, never fund, and hold the entire session's escrow hostage — at a cost of
~77k gas *(verified)*.

**Suggestions**, roughly in order of value:

1. **Add `unwind()`** callable from `Locked`, which returns `|net|` to each address with
   `_funded[party][ccy] == true` and moves the session to `Cancelled`. This is the missing settlement-failure
   procedure and it is a small amount of code (the per-party amounts are already in `_net`).
2. **Add a funding deadline.** Stamp `fundingDeadlineBlock` at lock; after it passes make `unwind()`
   *permissionless* so no participant depends on the admin being online. This is what makes the escrow
   trustless rather than admin-custodial.
3. **Allow partial funding.** Today `fund` transfers exactly `uint256(-n)` in one shot (`:200`), so a debtor
   one wei short delivers nothing. Change to `fund(uint8 currency, uint256 amount)` with a
   `_fundedAmount[party][ccy]` accumulator. Combined with (2) this also enables the standard fallback:
   at deadline, pay creditors *pro rata* out of whatever arrived instead of unwinding everything.
4. **Pre-fund or pre-authorise before lock.** The stronger design: require debtors to escrow (or at least
   to have balance + allowance) *before* `lockSession` accepts them, so a locked session is by
   construction settleable. Check `balanceOf`/`allowance` for every negative net inside `lockSession`
   and revert with the offending party, or exclude their obligations and re-net.
5. Expose a `fundingShortfall(party, currency)` view so the Settle UI can show who is blocking a session
   instead of just "not fully funded".

### R1-C2. Mint and redeem both price at the oracle mid, with no mint fee and no spread — a free option on the operator's buffer

*(verified)* — `src/CurrencyBucket.sol:140-156`, `:158-200`; `src/Oracle.sol:49-63`

`mint` charges nothing (`:140-156`, no fee term at all) and `redeem` charges only `feeBps` (50 bps as
deployed). Both use the same `oracle.ratePerUsd(currency)`. `Oracle.setRate` can be called any number of
times inside one MRW, and `_requireValidRateForCurrentWindow` only checks the *window nonce*, not
freshness or magnitude (`:249-252`). So an allowlisted party can mint at rate R and redeem at rate R′
inside the same window and keep the difference minus 50 bps.

Measured, with a 100,000 USD buffer, a 10,000 USD mint at 950 CLP/USD and a 5% CLP appreciation posted
in the same window:

```
PnL to user (50 bps redeem fee): +473.68 USD   (4.74% risk-free)
bucket reserve after:            99,473.68 USD  ← paid entirely out of the operator's buffer
```

Two things make this worse than a generic oracle-staleness issue:

- **The exposure is levered 10×.** `mintHeadroomUsd` (`:236-243`) is mathematically correct — a mint adds
  equal collateral and liability, so headroom is `10 × (collateral − 1.1 × liability)` — but the business
  consequence is that a buffer of `R` supports liability of `10R`. So the operator is writing a 10%-deep
  first-loss tranche across 10× its own capital.
- **The user chooses when to exercise.** Adverse moves they simply do not round-trip; favourable moves
  they do. A single mid rate for both directions is therefore a free straddle, not a fair two-way price.

Put together: at full utilisation, **one 10% favourable FX move round-tripped consumes the whole buffer**
(liability 1M against collateral 1.1M; a 10% move makes the claim 1.11M). Revenue at 50 bps on a full
exit of that book is 5,000 USD, so it takes roughly **20 full round-trips of the entire book to earn back
one such event**. That is not a viable risk/reward for the buffer provider.

This is not front-running-specific, but note that on a public mempool it *is* also a plain sandwich:
`setRate` is a visible transaction and mint/redeem are unrestricted within the window.

**Suggestions:**

1. **Charge a two-way spread, not a one-way fee.** Introduce `mintSpreadBps` / `redeemSpreadBps` (or
   `mintRate` / `redeemRate` as ideation.md §3 suggests) sized above plausible intra-window drift. This is
   the minimum change that stops the round-trip from being free.
2. **One rate per window.** Have `Oracle.setRate` revert if `_rateWindowNonce[currency] == hub.windowNonce()`
   — i.e. the rate is fixed for the life of a window. This kills the intra-window arb outright and matches
   the mental model the MRW already implies. If intra-window updates are wanted, gate them behind a
   max-deviation bound *and* a mint/redeem cooldown.
3. **Charge for the buffer.** A mint fee (or a periodic ticking fee on outstanding inventory) is what pays
   for the first-loss option currently given away. Redeem-only fees mean the operator earns on exit and
   carries risk on everything in between.
4. Per-bucket mint caps (already ideation.md Phase B item 1) reduce the *size* of the leak but not the
   *sign* of it.

## 3. High

### R1-H1. `eliminated` — the stated value proposition — is unauthenticated and self-reportable

*(verified for the duplicate-post half)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:168-185`, `:288-305`

`eliminated = grossPosted − Σ positive nets`. `postObligation` has no reference id, no dedupe, and no
creditor consent; a debtor may post against any allowlisted creditor. So one party controlling two
allowlisted addresses posts `A→B 1M` and `B→A 1M`, and the session reports:

```
gross = 2,000,000   residual = 0   eliminated = 2,000,000
```

for two transactions and no economic content. Duplicate identical rows are also accepted with no guard —
posting `alice→bob 100` twice yields a net of −200 *(verified)*.

Today this is a credibility problem (the number introduced in `cb95b96` as the "value prop of service"
cannot be quoted to a counterparty or an investor). If ideation.md Phase D item 10 ever bills on
`eliminated`, it becomes a direct fee-inflation / wash-trading vector.

**Suggestions:** add `bytes32 ref` to `Obligation` plus a `mapping(bytes32 => bool)` used-ref guard
(this also gives you invoice-level reconciliation, which the book currently cannot do at all); require
creditor acknowledgement before an obligation counts toward `gross` (see H2); and report `eliminated`
alongside participant count and distinct-counterparty count so a two-address wash is visible.

### R1-H2. Obligations are unilateral assertions — no creditor consent, no legal netting basis

`src/NettingSession.sol:168-185`

`postObligation` requires only that the poster is the debtor *or* the session admin. Nothing records that
the creditor agrees the debt exists, agrees the amount, or agrees to be netted.

Two distinct consequences, and the second is the one that matters commercially:

- **Admin can fabricate the book.** `SESSION_ADMIN_ROLE` can post any obligation between any two
  allowlisted parties (`:177`), which is enough to manufacture a debtor net that a party is then expected
  to fund, or a creditor net paid out of other parties' deliveries.
- **Multilateral netting without consent is not enforceable netting.** The whole economic point of a
  netting facility (CLS-style, or anything relying on close-out netting under a master agreement) is that
  the *net* legally discharges the *gross*, including in a counterparty's insolvency. That requires a
  netting agreement and per-session consent from each participant. Without it, a creditor who accepted
  `net − fee` on chain can still be pursued for the gross invoices off chain, and an insolvency
  administrator can cherry-pick. The on-chain book is then a payment-batching convenience, not netting.

ideation.md lists "counterparty acceptance" as a deferred product follow-up. I would re-rank it: it is
not polish, it is the thing that makes the product a netting service.

**Suggestions:** require the creditor to `acknowledge(index)` (or accept an EIP-712 signature from the
creditor at post time, so it stays one transaction); record a per-session `consentHash` per participant
referencing an off-chain master netting agreement, and require consent from every participant before
`lockSession` will include their rows; emit both so the audit trail shows *who agreed to what*.

### R1-H3. Receipt fee is read live at claim time, so it can be re-priced after parties have committed

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:146-150`, `:220`

`setFeeBps` has no phase guard and `claim` computes `Math.mulDiv(amount, feeBps, BPS)` from current
storage. So the admin can lock a session at 50 bps, let debtors deliver, then raise the fee to the 10% cap
before creditors claim:

```
bob's net: 100 CLP, expected 99.5 at lock
admin calls setFeeBps(1000) after fund, before claim
bob receives: 90 CLP
```

The same shape applies to `CurrencyBucket.setFeeBps` (`:95-99`) in front of a large pending `redeem`,
though there the user's exposure is one transaction rather than a multi-block commitment.

**Suggestions:** snapshot `feeBps` into a `sessionFeeBps` at `lockSession` and have `claim` read the
snapshot; reject `setFeeBps` unless phase ∈ {Idle, Settled, Cancelled}; and for the bucket, either
timelock fee increases or accept a user-supplied `maxFeeBps` / `minUsdOut` slippage argument on `redeem`.

### R1-H4. `lockSession` exceeds the block gas limit at a realistic book size

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:326-347`, `:349-357`, `:359-379`, `:288-297`

`lockSession` is `O(obligations + participants)` with a large constant: `_recomputeNets` walks every
obligation and then every participant × 3 currencies, and `_emitSessionVolume` calls the `O(N)`
`grossPosted` once per currency. `openSession`'s `_clearSessionState` then re-walks participants doing
9 `delete`s each, plus `delete _obligations`.

Measured on a participant chain (P participants, P−1 obligations):

| participants | `lockSession` gas | next `openSession` gas |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 1,588,352 | 824,809 |
| 150 | 4,371,152 | 1,857,809 |
| 250 | **6,853,952** | 2,290,809 |
| 500 | 13,960,952 | 5,173,309 |

Roughly 28k gas per participant to lock. Against a ~6.8M block gas limit (Rootstock's order of magnitude —
worth confirming for your target network) **the ceiling is around 250 participants** — and a multilateral netting book is exactly the product that wants hundreds of
participants and thousands of invoices. A separate measurement with 3,000 obligations between two parties
put `lockSession` at 10,044,878 gas *(verified)*.

Two failure modes, not one: the honest one (a real book simply cannot be locked), and the malicious one
(an allowlisted party spams cheap obligations at ~77k gas each until neither `lockSession` nor a later
`openSession` fits in a block — the state can only be cleared by the very call that is now too expensive).

**Suggestions:**

1. **Key session state by `sessionId`** — `_net[sessionId][party][ccy]`, `_obligations[sessionId]`, etc.
   Then `openSession` is `O(1)` and `_clearSessionState` disappears entirely, along with the
   clear-side DoS. This is the highest-value change here.
2. **Maintain nets incrementally in `postObligation`** (`_net[..] -= amount` / `+= amount` as rows arrive)
   so `lockSession` only has to freeze the phase, and accumulate `_grossPosted[currency]` and
   `_totalCreditorNets` / `_totalDebtorNets` the same way. That removes the `O(N)` loops from both `lock`
   and the `grossPosted`/`eliminated` views the UI polls.
3. Cap `obligationCount` per session and per poster; charge a deposit for posting if spam is a concern.

### R1-H5. `withdrawReserve` reads a possibly stale rate, in exactly the window where the rate cannot be refreshed

*(verified)* — `src/CurrencyBucket.sol:111-124`, `:217-223`; `src/ProtocolHub.sol:107-120`

`withdrawReserve` checks `collateralAfter >= 1.10 × liabilityUsd()`, but unlike `mint`/`redeem` it never
calls `_requireValidRateForCurrentWindow()`. The withdrawal window is by design the 100 blocks *after* the
MRW ends — and `Oracle.setRate` reverts while the window is closed (`Oracle.sol:53`). So the one path that
lets capital *leave* the vault is the one path guaranteed to price liabilities at a rate that can no longer
be updated:

```
window closed. CLP crashes 50% off chain.
oracle.setRate(1900e18) -> reverts WindowClosed
admin withdrawReserve(90,000 USD) against the stale 950 rate -> succeeds
```

Related, `liabilityUsd()` returns **0** when the rate is 0 (`:220-222`). Silent zero rather than a revert
means that if a bucket is ever upgraded to point at a fresh `Oracle`, liabilities read as zero while tokens
are outstanding and `withdrawReserve` will happily drain the entire vault.

**Suggestions:** require a rate stamped with the current-or-previous `windowNonce` *and* a max age in
`withdrawReserve`; make `liabilityUsd()` revert on a zero rate instead of returning 0; and require a
buffer above the floor to withdraw (e.g. coverage ≥ 115% after withdrawal) so the operator cannot sit
exactly on the 110% line.

## 4. Medium

### R1-M1. The reserve-withdrawal window is not actually a constraint

`src/ProtocolHub.sol:80-99`, `:107-120`; `src/CurrencyBucket.sol:111-124`

"Only during the first 100 blocks after MRW ends" reads as a safety property, but `HUB_ADMIN_ROLE` controls
`openWindow` and `closeWindow`, so the admin can manufacture a withdrawal window on demand:
`openWindow()` → `closeWindow()` → `withdrawReserve()`, three transactions, any time. Since hub admin and
bucket admin are the same key in `script/Deploy.s.sol:93-98`, the timing restriction binds nobody. Worth
either dropping the pretence or adding a real constraint (a timelock, or a minimum MRW duration that must
actually elapse before `closeWindow` is accepted).

### R1-M2. `fund` / `claim` do not re-check the allowlist — a de-listed party still settles

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:191-207`, `:209-234`

Already noted in ideation.md §4, but the compliance shape is worth stating plainly: a party allowlisted at
post time and de-listed (sanctioned, KYB lapsed, defaulted) before claim still receives escrow.

```
lock with alice -100 / bob +100
admin: hub.setAllowlist(bob, false)
alice funds, bob claims -> bob receives 99.5 CLP while not allowlisted
```

Note the tension with C1: naively adding an allowlist check to `fund`/`claim` converts a compliance
problem into a permanent deadlock, because a de-listed debtor can then never deliver and nobody can
unwind. **The two must be designed together** — which is another argument for shipping `unwind()` first.
A workable policy: block `claim` for de-listed parties, route the amount to an admin-controlled quarantine
balance rather than leaving it in escrow, and keep `fund` open so delivery obligations can still be met.

### R1-M3. Settlement nets are never emitted — the audit trail is missing its conclusion

`src/NettingSession.sol:123-133`, `:326-347`

The readme states prior sessions are reconstructible from events, but no event carries the **nets** — only
`ObligationPosted`, `Funded`, `Claimed`, `SessionVolume`. `_clearSessionState` then deletes `_net` on the
next `openSession`. So the actual settlement result — what each party owed or was owed after netting — is
only recoverable by re-running the netting algorithm off chain against the obligation events and trusting
that reimplementation. For a settlement product, that is the one number participants will want to prove
years later.

Also `_emitSessionVolume` skips any currency with `gross == 0` (`:352`), so absence of a `SessionVolume`
event is ambiguous between "no activity" and "not emitted".

**Suggestion:** emit `NetComputed(sessionId, party, currency, net)` for every participant at lock (or a
single event with the arrays), and emit `SessionVolume` for all three currencies unconditionally.

### R1-M4. Fee mechanics defeat the point of settling an invoice

`src/NettingSession.sol:209-234`

Two separate problems with charging the creditor a haircut on receipt:

- **The creditor is not paid in full.** A 100 CLP receivable settles at 99.5 CLP. In accounting terms the
  invoice is not discharged; there is a 0.5 CLP residual receivable, and the netting has to be followed by
  a write-off or a second collection. That is a strange output for a settlement system, and it interacts
  badly with H2 (a partial payment is weaker evidence of discharge than a full one).
- **Revenue is anti-correlated with value delivered.** The fee base is `residual`; the value metric is
  `eliminated = gross − residual`. The better the netting, the less the protocol earns, and a perfect cycle
  — maximum value delivered — is free. ideation.md accepts this for v0 and notes the structural-long
  asymmetry; the inverted-incentive framing is worth adding to that note, because it will shape which
  sessions the operator wants to run.

**Suggestion (small, keeps the v0 fee model):** have the debtor fund `|net| + feeOn(|net|)` and pay the
creditor the full `net`. Same revenue, same "debtors deliver, creditors receive" flow, but invoices settle
at face value. Longer term, a bps fee on `eliminated` charged to both sides — or a per-session/per-seat
fee — aligns price with the service; both need H1 fixed first or the fee base is gameable.

### R1-M5. `escrowBalance()` misreports session escrow, and the Settle UI displays it

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol:276-280`, `:46-47`; `app/src/App.tsx:813-816`

`escrowBalance()` returns the raw token balance, which also contains `accruedFees` carried over from prior
sessions (fees deliberately persist until withdrawn) and any tokens sent to the contract directly. The UI
renders `Escrow — CLP {escrowBalance} / need {totalDebtor}`, so a brand-new session with 0.5 CLP of
unwithdrawn fees displays `Escrow — CLP 0.5 / need 100`. The `fullyFunded` *gate* uses the internal
counters and is correct, so this is a reporting bug rather than a solvency bug — but it means the number
on screen and the number gating the button disagree.

**Suggestion:** return `_escrowReceived[c] - _escrowClaimed[c]`, and expose the raw balance separately as
`tokenBalance(c)` if it is wanted for reconciliation.

### R1-M6. A locked session has no expiry, so creditors carry open-ended FX and credit risk

`src/NettingSession.sol:123-133`

There is no deadline between `lockSession` and settlement. Nets are denominated in CLP/ARS token units, so
a creditor whose +60 CLP net sits unfunded for days is fully exposed to CLP moves with no cancellation
right, and (per C1) cannot walk away once anyone has funded. The same deadline that fixes C1 fixes this;
they are the same missing concept — a settlement cycle with a defined end.

### R1-M7. No per-participant exposure limits

`src/NettingSession.sol:168-185`

Nothing caps a participant's net position. Real netting facilities run bilateral and multilateral credit
limits per member precisely because a member's net delivery obligation is what everyone else's settlement
depends on. Here a party's net is whatever the book happens to produce, and (per C1) an unfundably large
net takes the whole session down. Suggest admin-set `maxNet[party][currency]` (or a single
`maxNetUsd` using the oracle), checked at lock, with over-limit rows excluded and re-netted.

### R1-M8. No cross-currency netting — the "FX" in FX netting is absent

*(verified)* — `src/NettingSession.sol` (no oracle dependency anywhere)

Netting is strictly per currency. A party who is +100 CLP and −100 USD must fund the USD leg in full and
claim the CLP leg separately:

```
alice CLP net: -100e18
alice USD net: +100e18
eliminated CLP: 0
```

The `NettingSession` never touches the `Oracle`, so the buckets provide convertibility and the session
provides netting, but the two never meet — the FX-relevant offset is exactly the one the product does not
capture. This is the biggest *product* gap after C1, and it is a genuine design decision rather than a
bug, so it deserves an explicit line in the readme's limitations (today a reader can reasonably assume
otherwise from "FX settlement network").

If it is wanted later: an opt-in numeraire mode that values every net in USD at a rate **stamped at lock**,
producing one USD-equivalent net per party with per-currency delivery. Note this imports oracle risk into
the netting product, which is currently oracle-free — a real trade-off, not a free upgrade.

### R1-M9. Trust model: a single EOA can mint unbacked settlement tokens and upgrade every contract

`script/Deploy.s.sol:93-98`; `src/SettlementToken.sol:19-35`; `src/CurrencyBucket.sol:254`

The deployer holds `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` on USD, ProtocolHub, both SettlementTokens, both CurrencyBuckets,
and NettingSession, plus `Ownable` on the Oracle. Two consequences beyond the usual "admin is trusted":

- `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` on `SettlementToken` can grant itself `ENGINE_ROLE` and mint **unbacked** CLP/ARS.
  The 110% invariant is enforced only on the `mint` *path* in the bucket; nothing enforces it against
  `totalSupply`, and there is no coverage check, no `isHealthy()`, and no breaker. So the bucket can sit
  undercollateralised indefinitely and the only consequence is that redeemers race.
- Every contract holding user funds is UUPS-upgradeable by that same key with no timelock, so the blanket
  approvals granted by the dApp (below) are approvals to mutable code.

ideation.md Phase B item 2 covers the breaker. What is missing is the *authority* split: at minimum
separate hub / bucket / session / token admin keys, put upgrades and `ENGINE_ROLE` grants behind a
timelocked multisig, and publish `coverageRatioBps()` per bucket so undercollateralisation is observable
by participants rather than only by the operator.

### R1-M10. dApp grants unlimited approvals to upgradeable contracts, on every action

`app/src/useProtocol.ts:698-707`, `:758-764`, `:786`

`approveSettlementToken` and `approveUsdMax`/`approveTokenMax` approve `1e9` tokens, and `fundSettlement`
re-approves before every single `fund` even when the existing allowance is sufficient. Given M9, that is an
unlimited allowance to code the operator can replace. For an enterprise settlement UI it should be the
opposite default: approve the exact `|net|` (which the app already knows from `netOf`), skip the approve
when `allowance >= amount`, and prefer EIP-2612 `permit` so funding is one signature and one transaction.

## 5. Low / hygiene

- **`Currency` loop bounds are tied to USD's position, not a count.** Every loop uses
  `for (uint8 c = 0; c <= Currency.USD; ++c)` (`NettingSession.sol:333`, `:341`, `:350`, `:370`, `:385`,
  `:393`) and validation uses `currency > Currency.USD`. Adding `BRL = 3` to `Currency.sol` would silently
  skip it in every loop while `postObligation` happily accepted it. Add `CURRENCY_COUNT` and use it
  everywhere; `USD` being the highest-numbered currency is load-bearing and undocumented.
- **Dead / redundant netting state.** Nets sum to zero per currency by construction, so
  `_totalDebtorNets[c] == _totalCreditorNets[c]` always. That makes `currencyFullyFunded`'s `need == 0`
  branch (`:284`) unreachable-as-written and the second loop in `_maybeMarkSettled` (`:393-397`) redundant
  with the first. One of the two totals can go.
- **No obligation revocation or amendment.** A mistyped row cannot be removed while the session is `Open`;
  the only remedy is `cancelSession` and re-posting the whole book. `revokeObligation(index)` by the
  original poster while `Open` would be cheap, and pairs naturally with the `ref` id from H1.
- **`collateralUsd()` is a view that can revert** (`CurrencyBucket.sol:210-215`, `InsufficientFeeBalance`).
  It should be unreachable given the current write paths, but if it ever trips it takes down `mint`,
  `redeem`, `withdrawReserve`, `mintHeadroomUsd`, `reserveUsd` and every UI read at once. Prefer saturating
  to 0 with a separate explicit health view.
- **Redeem fee is charged even on a shortfall partial fill** (`CurrencyBucket.sol:188`). A user taking a
  haircut in a redemption race still pays 50 bps, and the fee is taken out of collateral other holders'
  claims depend on. Consider waiving the fee when `partialRedeem == true`.
- **`cancelSession` works while the hub is paused** *(verified)* while `openSession` does not
  (`NettingSession.sol:111` vs `:136`). Probably deliberate, but the readme's pause table lists neither, so
  the intended pause semantics for session lifecycle calls are undocumented.
- **`_setAllowlist` emits `AllowlistUpdated` on no-op changes** (`ProtocolHub.sol:163-166`), which will
  double-count in any off-chain indexer that treats the event as a state transition.
- **`windowBlocksRemaining()` is off by one** (`ProtocolHub.sol:123-126`): at `block.number == windowEndBlock`
  the window is still open but the view returns 0.
- **No rotation path for the Oracle.** `Oracle` is non-upgradeable with a single `trustedOracle` EOA, and
  `CurrencyBucket` has no `setOracle`. Replacing a compromised or lost oracle key therefore requires a UUPS
  upgrade of both buckets. Add an admin-settable (ideally timelocked) `setOracle` on the bucket.
- **`Oracle.lastUpdated` is tracked but never enforced** anywhere — dead data until the staleness checks in
  ideation.md §3 land.

## 6. Test coverage gaps

The existing 36 tests are well-chosen for the happy path and the documented gates. What is untested is
almost exactly the list above:

- **No failure-path tests at all** for `NettingSession`: no test for a debtor who does not fund, no test
  that asserts escrow is recoverable, no test that a session can always be re-opened. C1 would have been
  caught by a single test asserting "after a partial fund, some path returns Alice's tokens".
- No test that mint→redeem round-trips are unprofitable across a rate change (C2).
- No test that fees cannot change between lock and claim (H3).
- No gas/scale test asserting `lockSession` fits in a block at target book size (H4).
- No test of `withdrawReserve` against a stale or zero rate (H5).
- No invariant/fuzz test for the two properties that actually define the system:
  `Σ net == 0` per currency, and `escrowReceived − escrowClaimed == Σ unclaimed positive nets`.
  Both are cheap to express as Foundry invariants and would cover a large class of future regressions.

The reproduction tests used for this review are in the session scratchpad
(`Probe.t.sol` … `Probe4.t.sol`) and can be dropped into `test/` as regression tests — they cover C1, C2,
H3, H4, M2 and M5 directly.

## 7. Suggested order of work

1. **`unwind()` + funding deadline + partial funding** (C1, M6). Nothing else matters while a routine
   settlement failure permanently traps funds. Design it together with the allowlist-recheck policy (M2)
   so the compliance fix cannot reintroduce a deadlock.
2. **Session state keyed by `sessionId`; nets and gross maintained incrementally** (H4). Removes the
   clear-side DoS and the block-gas ceiling, and makes the UI's `eliminated`/`grossPosted` polling cheap.
3. **Snapshot `feeBps` at lock; debtor funds `|net| + fee`** (H3, M4). Small, and it makes the settlement
   economics defensible to a counterparty.
4. **Two-way pricing: mint fee / spread, one rate per window, staleness + deviation bounds, and a rate
   freshness check in `withdrawReserve`** (C2, H5). This is ideation.md Phase B item 3, re-scoped from a
   data-quality item to a pricing one.
5. **Obligation `ref` id, dedupe, creditor acknowledgement, `NetComputed` events** (H1, H2, M3). This is
   what turns the book into an auditable netting record and makes `eliminated` a number you can quote.
6. **Authority split, timelocked upgrades, `coverageRatioBps()` + breaker, exact-amount approvals in the
   dApp** (M9, M10, and ideation.md Phase B item 2).
7. Then the known Phase B/C items: per-bucket caps, pause/queue instead of the redemption race, dual
   coverage reporting.

Items 1–3 are contained and high-value. Item 4 is a pricing decision as much as an engineering one and
probably needs a view from whoever provides the buffer capital. Item 5 is the one that changes what the
product *is*, and I would not let it slip much past the others.
